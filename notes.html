NMR Predictor Heuristics

This page documents the deliberately simple rules used by the teaching NMR predictor. It is designed to explain trends and spectra layout, not to replace curated or quantum-mechanics-based prediction software.

Scope

Group atoms first, predict shifts second, then render 1D and 2D teaching spectra.

Core Workflow

The predictor is a static browser app, so all chemistry is handled by lightweight JavaScript heuristics.

  • Parse SMILES into a graph of heavy atoms, bonds, aromatic flags, implicit hydrogens, and simple stereochemical markers.
  • Perceive common Kekule aromatic rings so uppercase benzene-like SMILES still behave as aromatic systems.
  • Group atoms by graph and environment keys before calculating ppm values. Grouping is not based on ppm similarity.
  • Assign base shifts from hand-written functional-group rules, then add small substituent and proximity corrections.
  • Expand 1H multiplets using simple n+1 binomial patterns. Component areas are normalized back to the signal integral.
  • Render 1D spectra as area-normalized Gaussian profiles plus centroid sticks. Broad exchangeable peaks use wider lines and therefore lower maxima.

Solvent

A solvent selector (CDCl3, DMSO-d6, D2O) adjusts exchangeable-proton shifts and visibility.

  • CDCl3 and DMSO-d6 use different flat-offset tables for OH/NH/SH shifts (e.g. amide N-H ~7.2 ppm in CDCl3 vs ~8.2 ppm in DMSO-d6), reflecting well-documented solvent-dependent hydrogen-bonding differences.
  • D2O hides all OH/NH/SH signals entirely, since they rapidly exchange with solvent deuterons and are not normally observed. This is a simplification — thiols exchange more slowly in practice than alcohols/amines/amides, but all three are lumped together here for a clear, consistent teaching point.
  • Non-exchangeable 1H and all 13C shifts are not solvent-adjusted.

Splitting And 2D Spectra

The couplings are intentionally simplified for teaching multiplicity.

  • Aliphatic 1H splitting uses adjacent carbon-bound hydrogens and binomial n+1 patterns.
  • Aromatic splitting only uses hydrogens on directly adjacent aromatic carbons. Non-equivalent adjacent environments can appear as dd-like patterns.
  • Alkene coupling uses approximate constants: trans ~16 Hz, cis ~10 Hz, unspecified ~12 Hz.
  • HSQC shows one-bond H-C correlations only.
  • COSY shows simple adjacent-proton correlations only.
  • NOESY uses CACTUS 3D coordinates when available, otherwise fallback coordinates. Off-diagonal peaks are proton pairs within 5 A, with relative volume approximately proportional to 1/r^6.
  • When real CACTUS 3D coordinates are available, a simplified through-space ring-current correction is added for atoms more than three bonds from an aromatic ring (e.g. across a fused ring system or a folded macrocycle). It is skipped for atoms within three bonds of a ring, since that region is already covered by the topological aromatic substituent table above, and skipped entirely when only fallback (non-physical) coordinates are available.

Known Limitations

These limitations are deliberate tradeoffs for a transparent teaching tool.

  • No full spin-system simulation, second-order effects, or dynamic exchange model. CH2 protons adjacent to a likely stereocenter are flagged in their label as "diastereotopic" and shown as one averaged signal, rather than being split into a real AB pattern.
  • Symmetry and equivalence are graph-environment based and approximate.
  • 13C intensities are shown as approximate carbon counts, not real relaxation or NOE-dependent intensities.
  • Exchangeable OH/NH/SH shifts are broad and solvent dependent; real peaks may move or disappear. The solvent selector covers CDCl3, DMSO-d6, and D2O only, with flat offsets rather than a full solvent model.
  • CACTUS atom order must match the parsed heavy-atom order before its coordinates are used for NOESY distances and the ring-current correction.
  • NOESY and the ring-current correction both use a single static 3D conformer (from CACTUS, or a non-physical fallback layout). Flexible chains in solution sample many conformations, so a real NOE is a population-weighted average that this single-geometry model cannot capture — predictions are most reliable for rigid or ring-constrained systems.
